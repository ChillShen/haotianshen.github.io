Illusory Shift

The logomark and typographic lockup are developed using halftone dot structures, creating a subtle sense of visual distortion and instability. This treatment introduces an illusory effect, where forms appear to shift and dissolve depending on scale and distance.

The approach draws inspiration from the cinematic styles of Christopher Nolan and Denis Villeneuve, whose work often explores perception, ambiguity, and the tension between clarity and distortion. These references are translated into a graphic system where typography and image are not fixed, but constantly in flux.

Visual System

The poster compositions are built upon a flexible visual foundation derived from the logomark system. Halftone structures are expanded and scaled to generate depth, texture, and variation across each layout.

Rather than relying on fixed compositions, the system allows elements to shift, overlap, and fragment. Typography interacts with image as part of the same visual field, creating a layered and unstable composition that reflects the illusory nature of perception.

Identity

The final identity is designed to function across both physical and digital contexts, from large-scale environmental graphics to printed materials and motion applications.

Built on a foundation of distortion and visual instability, the system maintains consistency while allowing variation in composition, scale, and format. Whether applied to posters, signage, or urban environments, the identity remains recognizable yet adaptable — reflecting the shifting and illusory nature of cinematic experience.
